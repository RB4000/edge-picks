How the numbers are made

Method

One model, written down in advance, run the same way every week. No hand-entered picks, no edited lines.

1. Efficiency: opponent-adjusted EPA per play

Every snap since {{ cfg.FIRST_SEASON }} carries an expected points added (EPA) value from nflverse: how much the play changed the offense's expected points. We average it per team per game, dropping late-game snaps where the outcome is already settled (win probability outside {{ (cfg.GARBAGE_WP[0]*100)|int }}–{{ (cfg.GARBAGE_WP[1]*100)|int }}% in the 4th quarter).

Raw EPA is misleading if you've played soft defenses, so a weighted ridge regression solves for every team's offensive and defensive rating at once. Each game's EPA/play is modeled as league average + offense rating + opponent defense rating + home/road term. Games are weighted by snaps and recency (half-life {{ cfg.HALF_LIFE_WEEKS|int }} weeks).

2. A prior instead of a blank slate

Each season starts from last season's final ratings, pulled {{ ((1 - cfg.PRIOR_REGRESSION) * 100)|int }}% back toward average to account for roster turnover. That prior carries the weight of about {{ (cfg.PRIOR_PLAYS / 62)|round|int }} games of data. By midseason the current year dominates.

3. Pace

The same regression structure, run on offensive plays per game, gives each team a pace rating for its offense and for what its defense allows. Projected plays × projected efficiency is what drives the total.

4. Points and home field

Projected efficiency and plays turn into points through a fit on every game since {{ cfg.FIRST_SEASON }}. Home field is estimated, not assumed: it's the average home margin in non-neutral games, with recent seasons weighted more. It moves as the league changes.

5. Picks

Model spread and total are compared with the market line. The difference is the edge, and the pick is whichever side the model prefers. Edges are grouped into tiers: under 2 points, 2–3.5, and 3.5+.

6. The rules

  • The first market line we capture for a game is frozen. Picks are graded against it, never against a later number.
  • A pick is generated and locked once, before kickoff, after every earlier game's play-by-play is in. It is never revised.
  • The ratings used for each week are published as a file next to the picks.
  • Closing lines are recorded after kickoff so closing line value can be measured.
  • The full ledger is published as CSV and committed to version control.

What the model doesn't know

Injuries, quarterback changes, weather, travel and motivation, except as they've already shown up in play-by-play. When the model disagrees sharply with the market, the market is often pricing exactly those things.
